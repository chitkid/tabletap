# UI/UX audit — TableTap, ahead of the redesign

Method: `ui-ux-pro-max` in audit mode, walking its ten priorities in order (Accessibility, Touch,
Performance, Style, Layout, Typography/Color, Animation, Forms, Navigation, Charts). Every surface
in `apps/web/app`, every component in `apps/web/components`, and the shared primitives in
`packages/ui/src/components` were read. `docs/screenshots/{landing,kitchen,admin,menu}.png` were
used as rendered evidence where a claim needed it; the live deployment was not reachable from this
environment, so findings marked **(computed)** are derived from the CSS constraint rather than
measured in a browser and name the width to verify at.

**55 findings.** Counts by priority are at the foot of this file.

## How to read the "RU" column

The product is about to be translated to Russian in full. Russian UI strings run 15–30 % longer than
English. **RU: latent** means the layout holds in English today and breaks once the strings change —
these have no substitute later, because once the strings are swapped the original is gone and the
break will read as a translation bug rather than as a constraint that was always there.
**RU: present** means it is already wrong and Russian makes it worse. **RU: —** means translation is
not a factor.

## What was checked and found clean

The brief named three areas as already worked hard. That holds up, and padding them would be worse
than saying so:

- **Colour and contrast.** The semantic pairings measure correctly on both surfaces:
  `--muted-foreground` on `--background` computes ≈ 4.94:1, `--border` on `--card` ≈ 3.70:1 (non-text
  threshold is 3:1), kitchen `--muted-foreground` on `--card` ≈ 8.09:1. `status-badge.tsx:26-34`
  documents the one pairing that needed a per-surface override and `packages/ui/src/tokens.test.ts`
  holds it. The CI token gate is doing its job — there is no raw hex and no bare duration anywhere in
  `apps/` or `packages/ui/src`.
- **Reduced motion.** `globals.css:23-33` collapses durations _and delays_ product-wide in one rule.
  Collapsing the delays is the part most implementations miss, and the comment explains why. No
  component writes a competing branch. Nothing to add.
- **Motion technique.** `@starting-style` (`entrance.tsx:30-31`, `ticket-card.tsx:25`,
  `row-editor.tsx:27`) keeps the served HTML free of `opacity-0`, so a browser that never ran the JS
  is not handed an invisible page. Transitions are `transform`/`opacity` only. The stagger caps at
  the fourth child (`entrance.tsx:34`). This is correct and unusually careful.
- **Focus management on the surfaces that have it.** `basket-sheet.tsx:50-55`,
  `ticket-card.tsx:53-72`, `menu-row.tsx:110-112`, `qr-actions.tsx:43-46` all hand focus back
  deliberately when the control that was pressed is unmounted. Findings **F2** below are the three
  places this discipline is _not_ applied, not a claim that it is absent.
- `dish-card.tsx:38` uses `alt=""` on the dish photo. That is right, not a finding: the `<h3>`
  beside it names the dish, and the `Plate` fallback is marked `decorative` for the same reason.

## The three I would fix first

**1. `lang="en"` and the four hardcoded-locale sites, before the strings are swapped —
`apps/web/app/layout.tsx:44`, `apps/web/lib/money.ts:10`, `apps/web/components/admin/week-bars.tsx:11,17`,
plus the two binary plurals at `basket-bar.tsx:36` and `week-bars.tsx:27`.** This is the cheapest
change in the document and the only one whose cost multiplies if it is done afterwards. `lang="en"`
on a Russian document makes every screen reader in the product pronounce Cyrillic through an English
voice — and Lighthouse will keep reporting accessibility 100 throughout, because `html-has-lang`
checks only that the attribute is present and well-formed. The plural handling is the same story:
`count === 1 ? 'item' : 'items'` has exactly two branches where Russian needs three, so once the
strings are extracted the grammar is wrong for two counts in five and the fix has become a hunt
through the message catalogue instead of a two-line change at the source.

**2. The landing's three cards and its off-screen proof —
`apps/web/components/landing/landing-content.tsx:133-206`.** Three cards with identical structure
(S1), two of them carrying ~350 px of dead space because the QR sets the row height (S2), and a
"Simulate rush" button whose twelve orders land on a board the visitor is not looking at (S3). These
are one problem, not three: the page has nothing to _show_, so it describes instead, and the
description has nowhere to go but a grey paragraph inside a grey card. The fix is a single
structural move — put a live miniature of the kitchen board on the landing, subscribed to the same
socket, and let the rush button drive it. That converts the page from description to demonstration,
fills the void that currently defines the Kitchen and Admin cards, and gives the three roles visibly
different shapes as a by-product. It also fixes the one thing a stranger currently cannot learn from
this page: that the product is fast.

**3. `whitespace-nowrap` + `shrink-0` in `packages/ui/src/components/button.tsx:8`, and its four
dependent call sites — `ticket-card.tsx:119-149`, `tables-table.tsx:267-271`, `basket-bar.tsx:32-41`,
`demo-terminal.tsx:123-135`.** One declaration in the base button means no button in the product may
wrap or shrink, anywhere. Today that is fine because English is short and the tight spots are
documented as tight — two of those four call sites carry comments saying so in as many words. After
the translation it is four layout breaks, the worst of them on the kitchen board, where the confirm
group at `ticket-card.tsx:123` has no `flex-wrap` at all and already fills its column in English at
1280 px. The repair pattern exists in this repo — `qr-actions.tsx:79,85` solves exactly this with
`flex-wrap` and `basis-full` — it simply was not applied here. Fixing the base plus four call sites
retires the whole latent class while the English strings are still on screen to check it against,
which is the only window in which this work is cheap.
